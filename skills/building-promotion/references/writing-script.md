# Writing Script

## Overview

What the film says inside its structure's blocks, and how the voice, captions and picture divide it: the viewer, the seven jobs and the trust beat, the core line and anchor phrase, sold vs shown, from blocks to beats, hook lines, line formulas, writing for the ear, the line budget, coverage and gaps, brand touches, claims, the offer and CTA, the muted read and the honesty scan for words. The structure, its blocks and their lengths come from [choosing-structure.md](./choosing-structure.md). The **Message plan** and **Beat sheet** in [creating-promotion.md](./creating-promotion.md) steps 3–4 are written to both.

## Guidelines

### The viewer

- One watch, on a phone, half-attending, often muted. The film lands when that viewer can say who it's for, what they get, what's sold, and what to do next.
- With broad ad audiences, the "this is for me" cue in the first 3 s does the targeting: name the trade or role in its own label.
- The viewer may not decide (staff, a family member). Name the decider's role in the call-out ("clinic owners") so they know whom to tag or forward; the film still asks for one action. Where the trade's own customers order by message (a florist, a bakery), naming the owner also keeps those customers from answering the ad to order.

### The seven jobs and the trust beat

The **Message plan** fills one line per row from **Study**, in the audience's own words, and names the structure's block that owns it. Tag every beat-sheet line with its job. The rows are a checklist; the structure sets their order ([choosing-structure.md](./choosing-structure.md) → **The catalog**): each must be true in the viewer's head before they act.

| # | Job | The viewer must know | Owned by | When missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Who | This is for me: my trade or role | The promise block: picture + caption + voice | Wrong people stay; right people scroll |
| 2 | Pain and trigger | The problem, and when it bites | The old way's artifact in the picture + the line that names it | No reason to keep watching |
| 3 | What it does for you | Before → after, in their terms | The picture proves it (the app performing); a voice line or caption says the consequence | No desire |
| 4 | What is sold | "We build one like this for you" when sold ≠ shown; else the product and where to get it | Voice, then a caption that names the builder, in the block marked B | Read as an app to download |
| 5 | Offer | What it costs me | Voice or caption in the block marked O, and the end card | "Free? What's the catch?" |
| 6 | Next step | What happens if I act: small and safe | Voice | Acting feels like a commitment |
| 7 | CTA | What to do | Voice + button: the button's verb and channel | Watched, not acted on |
| T | Trust beat | Who builds it, and why believe them; when sold = shown, proof about the product | Voice (the who line, any named proof from the Brief) + those names written on screen | "Who are these people?" |

- **The picture proves; the voice says what it can't.** That is the meaning of a beat in the viewer's words, and the seller and offer facts (jobs 4–7 and T). How much the voice carries is the voice mode ([directing-voice.md](./directing-voice.md) → **Coverage**). In every mode a line about the UI states its consequence, never a button or a step.
- Jobs 2 and 3 are required in every film: the problem and a consequence, each in the audience's words.

### Cutting to fit

When the slots run out, cut per [choosing-structure.md](./choosing-structure.md) → **Scaling and cut order**. The promise, one proof, the brand cue, the ask and, when sold ≠ shown, the sold line stay in every film; one that loses its voice slot moves to a caption or the end card.

- In a short cut the trust beat shrinks to the who line or the disclosure line; named proof needs a calm cut ≥ 25 s. The brand's floor (**Brand touches**) holds at every length.

### Core line, anchor phrase, value frame

- **Core line:** "[who] [gets the outcome / stops the pain] with [what's sold]", in the audience's own nouns from **Study**, with no product jargon.
- **Anchor phrase:** the core line in ≤ 4 words. It appears ≥ 3 times across voice, large type and the UI result: once in large type, twice spoken; with no voice, three times on screen. Mass-market and low-literacy audiences get identical words each time; pro audiences may paraphrase. The hook's word returns at the payoff as a callback.
- **Value frame:**

  | Frame | Example | Fits |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Relief | "No more copying the time book" | Informational products; problem-aware viewers; a visible old way |
  | Outcome | "Every cake order on one board" | The result shows in one frame; solution-aware viewers |
  | Feature | "Snap the sheet; it reads every name" | A new mechanism; developers |
  | Identity | "You are the art director" | Creative, social or status products |

  Informational products put relief in the hook and the outcome in the payoff. Show the feature; say the relief or the outcome.

### Sold vs shown

| Sold | Shown | Sold line | Disclosure line | CTA asks for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The product | The product | — | — | The product |
| A build (studio) | A sample app | "We'll build you an app like this." | "Sample app · built by <Seller>" | A build conversation |
| A template | The template with sample data | "Start from this; make it yours." | "Template" | The template |
| A platform or no-code tool | An app made with it | "Made with <Platform>." | "Made with <Platform>" | Sign-up |
| A done-for-you service | The tool the service runs | "We run <task>; you see it here." | "<Seller> does this for you" | Contact |

Never show the shown thing's own CTA, such as a store badge. Where the disclosure line goes: [promotion-contract.md](./promotion-contract.md) → **Disclosure**.

When sold ≠ shown:

| Beat | Lands | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Builder named | At the reveal, the app's first appearance | The disclosure line, then the who line |
| "We build one like this for you" | In the block marked B, on its first app beat; never only at the end | Voice + a matching caption |
| Offer | In the block marked O | Voice or a caption, and the end card |

An offer that lands before the sold line says whose it is ("Your app's first version, free."). A bare "the first version is free" before the viewer knows what is built confuses a cold viewer.

### From blocks to beats

Lay the structure's blocks out to the length per [choosing-structure.md](./choosing-structure.md) → **Length**; each block becomes one or more beats. Whatever the structure, these lines keep their own rules:

| Line | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | **Hook lines**; it carries the promise in the form the structure marks P |
| Device / reveal | The signature motion; in a music-led film, no voice over the drop + the device's first bar |
| Sold line | In the block marked B (**Sold vs shown**) |
| What it does for you | Over the app performing |
| Who | One line, ≤ 10 syllables, in a caption-free window or over a caption that says the same; never the disclosure line read aloud in full |
| Proof | Only from the Brief's **Trust and proof**: one line, ≤ 12 syllables, in a caption-free window, its names written |
| Close | One line, ≤ 12 syllables: the button's verb + the next step; never across the ending hit |

- The number of voice lines follows the voice mode and the blocks ([directing-voice.md](./directing-voice.md) → **Coverage**).
- Fit the close to its slot per [directing-voice.md](./directing-voice.md) → **Ending**; a short slot takes the short form ("Message us.").
- Muted-first placements (4:5, 1:1) get a picture hook, never a question, and the offer as a caption.

### Hook lines

- Frame 0 reads in 0.25 s ([directing-picture.md](./directing-picture.md) → **Frame 0 and the cover**); ≤ 7 words on screen before 3.0 s, hook text and label together.
- The first line starts at 0.1–1.5 s (a type-device hook may let the type go first), ends ≤ 2.9 s, and holds ≤ 11 syllables. It never opens with "Introducing" or the brand name.
- Write 3 hooks of different types ([choosing-style.md](./choosing-style.md) → **Hook types**), each ending on the same frame at 3.0 ± 0.1 s, so the body after it is identical.
- Second-language and code-switching audiences get the hook's meaning in the picture, with no wordplay.
- Whether the hook is voiced: [directing-voice.md](./directing-voice.md) → **Coverage**.

### Line formulas

| Line | Formula | Good | Bad, and why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | [Trade call-out]? + [their workaround or pain, in their words] | "Bakers: still taking cake orders in the chat?" · "Payroll by hand again?" | "Want to grow your business faster?" (a speed promise; no trade) · "Introducing <Name>, the smart payroll solution." (opens on the brand; no pain) |
| Problem / agitate | [What the old way costs them], in their words, over its artifact | "Every Friday, the time book and a calculator." | "Manual processes are inefficient." (no picture, not their words) |
| A beat's meaning (narrated) | [What changes for them] at that beat, over the app doing it | "Every add-on reaches the customer's phone first." | "Tap Send for approval." (a step) · "It's that easy!" (ease claim) |
| Offer | [Free, or the price] + [exactly what] + [whose] | "Your app's first version, free." · a plate on the reveal: "First version free." | "FREE!" (free what?) · "Free app!" (heard as free forever) · "$0 to build" (a price-like number) |
| Service / sold | [We] + [future build verb] + [you] + [like this] | "We'll build you an app like this." | "An app like this, for your clinic." as the only sold line (no builder: reads as a download) · "<Seller> builds apps like this for contractors." (third person and plural: a catalogue, not yours) |
| Who | [Made by] + [the seller's name] | "Made by <Seller>." | "This is <Seller>'s app." (a product to get) |
| What it does for you | [Their old way, in their words] → [the result they get], over the picture that proves it | "Your logbook, now payslips." · "Chat orders, one board." | "Tap Compute to see the total." (narrates a step) · a sample UI number voiced as a claim |
| Proof | [We also made] + [named, checkable work] | "We also built <App A> and <App B>." with both names written | "Trusted by hundreds of businesses." (unsourced) · any count not in **Proof** |
| Close | [The button's verb] + [us] + [the next step] | "Message us, let's talk first." · short slot: "Message us." | "Click the link below." (not the button's verb) · "We reply in minutes!" (reply speed) · "Only 3 slots left!" (invented scarcity) |
| Why now | Only a real date or a real limit | "Year-end payslips: due Dec 31." | "Today only!" (invented urgency) |

### Writing for the ear

| Rule | Good | Bad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One idea per breath group (limits in **Rate and budget**) | "Your app's first version, free." | "We at <Seller> will build you an app just like this one, made for your business." |
| End on the stressed word | "We'll build one for your clinic." | "For your clinic, we'll build one." |
| A future build verb for a service | "We'll build you…" | "We have an app for that." (reads as a product) |
| Whose it is, in an early offer | "Your app's first version, free." | "First version free." alone before the reveal |
| Clusivity: where the language marks it, the seller is the exclusive "we" and the shared next step the inclusive "we" | "Message **us**, **let's** talk." | "Let's build your app." (the inclusive form for what only the seller does) |
| The consequence, not the step | "Done." | "Press Send." |
| The UI's own verb, never a stronger one; the anchor stays in the UI's language, even mid code-switch | "notified" · "AI draft" | "knows" · "written" |
| A state line can't be heard as a command | "Booked." | "Book now." |
| Names, numbers and URLs written on screen while spoken | Proof names in a free caption band | A voiced URL beyond a short domain |

- Language, code-switching and address: [directing-voice.md](./directing-voice.md) → **Language, code-switch, address**; how names and numbers are said: **Pronunciation and numbers**.

### Rate and budget

1. **Before casting**, plan at 3.5 syllables a second (a start value). A language budgeted in characters or morae starts from a native reference read ([directing-voice.md](./directing-voice.md) → **Pace**).
2. **Measure the chosen voice** on its takes (casting-test takes count): text syllables ÷ the span measured from the audio. Group the takes by read: one flowing clause; two breath groups; a short tag ≤ 6 syllables; a warm offer or CTA.
3. **Fit a duration model** on those takes: seconds = a × syllables + b × breath groups. Use it only inside the syllable range it was fit on.
4. **The planning rate** for each new line is the slower of the model and the slowest measured pace for its read. A kept take uses its measured span.
5. **Read every line aloud to the grid** at the planning rate, against the film's click, before any take.
6. A line over its slot loses words. Never plan a slower read or a stretch.

- Breath groups: ≤ 13 syllables each, ≤ 2 a line; ≤ 11 syllables for a line that must fit before the drop.
- Plan by read, not by energy: energy sets the gaps and the reads, not the pace.
- No voice-share or density target; both follow from the slots. When the user has rejected a film as too dense, keep new films below its syllables per second of runtime.
- Budget most languages in syllables; English in words or syllables (start at 2.5 words a second, the low end of the 2.5–3.0 broadcast norm); Chinese, Japanese and Thai in characters or morae, at the voice's measured rate. Never carry a words-a-second rule across languages.

### Coverage and gaps

| Rule | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Replace, don't add | A voice line takes the slot of a line that would echo a caption; the one echo left is a verbatim hook (**Voice, captions, picture**) |
| Gaps by energy ([choosing-style.md](./choosing-style.md) → **Energy scale**) | E1–3: ≥ ½ beat between lines. E4–5: ≥ 1/8 note inside one thought, ≥ 1 beat between thoughts. Never isolated statements 2–5 s apart |
| Voice-free floor | Always music-only: the drop downbeat + 1 beat, the device's first bar, the device's motion (a fill, a spin, a morph), fills and risers, the ending hit. Key lines and none: at least one stretch ≥ 3 s for the device. Narrated: music-only breaths of 1–2 s, each after a line that framed what to watch |
| No line across | A drop, a device bar, the ending hit, or a key effect |

- Whether to voice at all: [directing-voice.md](./directing-voice.md) → **Coverage**. Where each line sits on the grid: **Placement on the grid**.

### Voice, captions, picture

| Channel | Carries | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picture / UI | The pain's artifact; the app performing; its results; the sample and AI labels | A value or step the source lacks |
| Captions | The hook phrase; each narrated beat's key word; the offer (a plate, or a caption swapped to it); the line naming the builder; the close when it sits before the hit | A transcript; a different sentence from the one the voice is saying |
| Labels | ≤ 4 words in the app's language; the noun is the anchor | A sentence |
| Voice | Key lines: hook, sold line, who, close (CTA + next step), and the offer. Narrated: also the problem and each beat's meaning. Always: seller and offer facts the picture can't show | Per [promotion-contract.md](./promotion-contract.md) → **Honesty rules** |
| End card | The brand, the offer's short form, the button, the URL, the disclosure | Per [directing-picture.md](./directing-picture.md) → **End card** |

| Caption mode | What it is | Values | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Key-phrase (default) | The caption holds the line's key phrase; the voice says that sentence | 2–7 words | The voice saying another sentence while it shows |
| Free the slot | To place a seller line where a caption stands, shorten, empty or rewrite that caption so voice and screen match; list it as a picture change | — | Talking over the old caption |
| Verbatim | Voice = caption | ≤ 1 a film: the hook | Mid-film |
| Complementary | The screen gives a number or a one-word label; the voice gives its meaning, or a line containing the label's word | — | A voice line that turns the label into a promise |
| Voice-only | No caption at that moment | ≤ 3 a film; an offer or CTA said voice-only also appears on the card or an earlier caption | Proper names (write them) |

- Voice and captions share one language. A second language is a second cut, re-budgeted for text expansion.

**Reading budget per window.** Words the voice says are free; a number counts as 2 words. With a caption, N_read ≤ (0.6 × settled seconds − T_gist) ÷ t_word, where settled seconds is the window's still time; without one, drop the 0.6. T_gist = 1.1 s fluent, 1.4 s older or second-language, + 0.4 s after a hard cut.

| t_word, seconds per unvoiced word (px at 1080 wide) | 38 px | 29 px |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fluent | 0.25 | 0.29 |
| Moderate, a daily app user | 0.30 | 0.35 |
| Low-tech or second-language | 0.36 | 0.41 |

- Captions run ≤ 3 words a second. Chat floods, handwriting and app text that read as texture are exempt; words the viewer needs in order to act (the offer, the builder, the CTA, the URL) never are.
- Rates by language: reading from Brysbaert 2019, Table 5 (English 236 words a minute, Finnish 195, Arabic 181); caption speed and line length from the Netflix timed-text guide for the caption language (English 20 characters a second; German 17; Spanish 17, 42 a line; Korean 12; Chinese 9; Japanese 4, 13 full-width a line). Chinese, Japanese and Thai are budgeted in characters.
- Caption lines fit ≤ 950 px in the rendered font ([directing-picture.md](./directing-picture.md) → **Type, sizes, colour, contrast**).

### Brand touches

| Touch | When | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seen | By ~3–5 s | A brand-kit cue in frame ([directing-picture.md](./directing-picture.md) → **Brand kit**) |
| Named in words | At the reveal | The disclosure line, then the who line |
| Heard | ≥ 1× when voiced | The who line; in a cut with no who line, the close ("Message <Seller>."). With no voice: the name seen twice, and the seller's sonic cue |
| Present at the peak | The drop, the device, the payoff | On screen while the emotion peaks, not only after it: a peak after the brand appears goes with intent, one before it doesn't |
| End card | The last beat | [directing-picture.md](./directing-picture.md) → **End card** |

Short touches inside the story, never a logo card.

### Claims

A claim is a distinct pain, capability, benefit, number, guarantee, price or term, counted when voiced, captioned, labelled or titled. A repeat isn't a new claim; incidental UI text isn't counted but must be true.

- Claims by length: ≤ 15 s → 3 (pain, outcome, offer); 21–25 s → 4; 26–34 s → 5; a longer film adds a claim only with a block that proves it. Never more than 2 per 10 s, or 1 per line. One fewer for SMB, mass-market and second-language audiences.
- A film holds 1 pain, the outcome, ≤ 2 supports each proven on screen at that moment, and 1 offer.
- Cut in this order: claims outside the one task; claims with no picture at that moment; second offer terms; pains beyond the first; unmeasured adjectives ("fast"); a support that competes with the outcome. Never cut the outcome, the sold line, the offer or the CTA.

### Offer and CTA

- **One offer, ≤ 6 words:** "Download <Name> free" (the cold default), "First 10 orders on us", a real guarantee from **Proof**, or a price only when the price is the hook. Said or captioned in the block marked O (**Sold vs shown**), and again on the end card.
- Name exactly what is free; never a bare "Free". The wording never implies free forever ("Free app"). Where a market's law wants a free offer's limits beside it (Japan, Mexico), put the limit on screen next to the offer.
- Terms and conditions go to the landing page or the reply, never into the film. Risk: a free-first offer can be heard as "free to keep"; the end card carries its exactly-true form.
- The spoken verb is the button's verb. One destination per film. Offer and CTA lines together take ≤ 25 % of the runtime.

| Destination | Spoken | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| App store | "Download <Name> free" | Store badges |
| Website | "Try it free at <short domain>" | URL |
| Chat (a message button) | "Message us" + the next step | The button: "Message us on <channel>" |
| Organic TikTok / Instagram | "Link in bio", or "Message <Seller> on <channel>" | Handle |
| Enterprise | "Book a 15-minute demo" | "Book a demo" |

### The muted read

Write the captions, labels and end card in order, with no voice. They alone must read, in the structure's order: the promise → what it does → who builds it → the offer → what to tap. Muted, the music's clock is gone, so the words and the picture carry the meaning on their own.

- The disclosure line alone, at its small size, doesn't carry the builder: a caption-size line, or the end card's disclosure line, must.
- Checked on paper at G2 and on the muted render at G3 ([checking-promotion.md](./checking-promotion.md) → **G2 Script budget**, **G3 Measured**).

### Honesty scan for words

Back-translate every line, caption, label and card that isn't in English literally, then scan them all for:

| Risk | Watch for | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | "instantly", "in seconds", "right away", "fast" | Only when the speed plays at real speed on screen, or is in **Proof**; never voiced |
| Absolute or guarantee | "never miss", "guaranteed", "no mistakes" | Only from **Proof** |
| Savings or outcome | "save", "earn more", "worth it" | Only from **Proof**; never voiced |
| Free and price-like numbers | "free", "gratis", "$0", "0 fees" | Say in words what is free, never "forever" (**Offer and CTA**); a price form only when the price is the offer |
| Ease | "just", "only one" | Only when the sample shows it; ≤ 1 a film; vary it across a series |
| Completion | "done", "already" | A state, never beside a time word ("done in seconds") |
| Scarcity and urgency | "limited slots", "today only" | Only a real date or limit |
| Reply speed or person | "we reply right away", "a real person answers" | Only what the Brief says the seller does |

Then check the Brief's **Never say** and [promotion-contract.md](./promotion-contract.md) → **Honesty rules**.

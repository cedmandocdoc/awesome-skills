# Directing Promo Video

## Overview

The craft every campaign is planned, built and checked against: message, tone, concept, timeline, lines, picture, sound, and the checks before the user sees a draft. The viewer watches once, on a phone, half-attending and often muted. A campaign lands when that viewer can repeat one idea: who it's for, what they get, what's sold, and what to do next.

Every px value is for a 1080-wide frame. For other widths, scale by width ÷ 1080.

## Guidelines

### Message

- **Core line:** "[who] [get outcome / stop pain] with [what's sold]", in the audience's own nouns from **Study**, with no product jargon.
- **Anchor phrase:** the core line in ≤ 4 words. It appears ≥ 3 times across voice, large type and the UI result: once in large type, twice spoken. Mass-market and low-literacy audiences get identical words each time; pro audiences may paraphrase.
- **Value frame:**

  | Frame | Example | Fits |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Relief | "No more recopying the time book" | Informational products; problem-aware viewers; a visible old way |
  | Outcome | "Payslips out before lunch" | The result shows in one frame; solution-aware viewers |
  | Feature | "Snap the sheet; it reads every name" | A new mechanism; developers |
  | Identity | "You are the art director" | Creative, social or status products |

  Informational products put relief in the hook and the outcome in the payoff. Show the feature; say the relief or the outcome.
- **Sold vs shown:**

  | Sold | Shown | Said by 6.5 s | Label on every product frame | CTA asks for |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | The product | The product | — | — | The product |
  | A build (studio) | A sample app | "[Seller] builds an app like this for your [trade]" | "Sample app built by [Seller]" | A build conversation |
  | A template | The template with sample data | "Start from this, make it yours" | "Template" | The template |
  | A platform or no-code tool | An app made with it | "Made with [Platform]" | "Made with [Platform]" | Sign-up |
  | A done-for-you service | The tool the service runs | "We run [task]; you see it here" | "[Seller] does this for you" | Contact |

  Never show the shown thing's own CTA, such as a store badge.
- Tag every line as core, support, step, or sold/offer.

### Tone

Pick one tone by the first rule that fits; rules 6–7 always apply.

1. **Risk:** money, health, legal matters, records and operations get calm or warm-plain. Humour goes on the pain, never on the claim.
2. **Audience:** low-literacy, older or second-language viewers get warm-plain, or local-mass where the culture's mass ads run that way and a native speaker checks the script. Developers: calm or witty. Enterprise buyers: calm or premium. Young consumers: energetic or witty.
3. **Motive:** transformational → energetic, premium or witty; informational → calm or warm-plain.
4. **Platform:** TikTok and Reels get native polish; LinkedIn and YouTube get produced.
5. **Stage:** a cold campaign may lift the energy one step, for the hook only.
6. One of the three concepts is a one-step tone mismatch.
7. When sold ≠ shown, the seller's voice owns the narration and the end card, within one tone step of the product's.

| | Calm-trustworthy | Warm-plain | Witty | Premium-cinematic | Energetic-playful | Local-mass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Pace (× the language's normal rate) | 0.70–0.80 | 0.75–0.85 | 0.80–0.90 | 0.70–0.80 | 0.80–0.90 | 0.80–0.90 |
| Music family | Soft piano, warm pads, light strings or guitar | Acoustic folk-pop: guitar, ukulele, claps | Pizzicato, marimba or indie; or near-silence plus stingers | Ambient electronic, swelling pads, low piano | Electronic pop, house, hip-hop | Local pop, or a sung jingle |
| BPM | 75–100 | 90–115 | 95–125 | 70–100, or free | 100–130 | 100–130 |
| Beat under the voice | Soft or none | Light | Bouncy, stops for punchlines | Weak | Strong only where nobody speaks | Strong; a jingle is the clock |
| Energy curve | Flat, small lift at the end | Gentle rise to the offer | Stop–start, silence before jokes | One swell at the reveal, then resolve | High | High throughout |
| Camera / UI moves | 0.5–0.7 s ease in-out, no springs | 0.45–0.6 s, springs damped ≥ 0.85 | Action 0.3–0.45 s, reaction held | Camera 0.8–1.5 s | 0.25–0.4 s, no springs on UI | 0.3–0.5 s; the core line slams in |

| Polish | Produced | Native-leaning | Native | Produced | Either | Produced, or the local trend |

- Inside a moment, **Holds** set the time in every tone.
- Outside the moments, a cut faster than 2 s needs one message, a picture-only voiced change or ≤ 4 new words at 8–11% of frame height, and one world or a cause.
- Low literacy means few big words and repetition, not slow cuts.
- The first 5 s show ≥ 3 visual states, whatever the tone.
- The Spec records the tone's name and its row as numbers, with any overrides and the humour target.

### Concept and look

| Concept | Needs | Fits | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem → solution | A visible old way; one task | Informational; problem-aware SMB; cold | The old way is invisible |
| Before / after | An after-state readable in one frame | Low literacy; sound off; the ≤ 15 s cut | The change needs reading |
| Result first | A striking result that reads at phone size | Cold; hook variants | The result isn't visual |
| Question with the input | A real input the viewer owns | Cold hooks | The input is abstract |
| Demo walkthrough | One task in ≤ 3 screens | Solution-aware; dev or pro; warm | The task is unfamiliar |
| Day in the life | A person or a time device | Consumer, SMB; ≥ 30 s | The ≤ 15 s cut |
| Comparison of steps or time | Real counts in **Proof** | High involvement; dev tools | No measured numbers |
| Testimonial | A consenting customer's quote in **Proof**, as text | Warm | No quote |
| Jingle / mnemonic | A sung line | Local-mass | No native writer |
| Type-led | Nothing | Energetic or premium; sound off | Low literacy |
| Skit | An illustrated character | Witty; TikTok | No character |

| Look | Use |
| --- | --- |
| UI motion graphics, rebuilt from the source | The default |
| An object world of the old way in HTML/CSS (paper, notebook, chat) | The problem world |
| Kinetic type as narrator | Sound-off viewers |
| Stylized or 3D | Premium tone only |

- **People:** on-screen text as narrator with a second-person voice by default; an illustrated person or mascot for the witty, warm and local tones; a stock setting or hands in the hook only.
- **Choose by awareness:** problem-aware → problem → solution or before/after; solution-aware → result first, demo or comparison; unaware or mass → day in the life, jingle or skit. Defaults: problem → solution or result first for the master; before/after or result first for the cut.
- **Worlds:** at most 3, old way → product → the seller's type world, each entered through an object.
- **Two brands:** the product world uses the design doc's tokens exactly, status colours included. The seller owns captions, titles, the label, the sold line and the end card. The seller's type system stays constant across worlds.

### Timeline

| Beat | 21 s master (2 moments) | 30 s master (3 moments) | ≤ 15 s cut (1 moment) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook: product and who on screen by 3.0 s | 0–3 | 0–3 | 0–3 |
| Sold line over a still frame, said by 6.5 s | 3–6 | 3–6 | 3–6 |
| Moment 1 (its push-in starts after the sold line) | 6–11 | 6–11.5 | — |
| Moment 2 | 11–16.5 | 11.5–17 | 6–11.5: the result |
| Moment 3 | — | 17–22.5 | — |
| Restate | — | 22.5–24 | — |
| Offer line over the still, pulled-back result | — | 24–26 | — |
| End card | 16.5–21 | 26–30 | 11.5–15 |

- Length = hook + sold beat + Σ moments (from **Holds**) + restate + offer beat, within 21–34 s. Over 34 s → cut a moment. Never pad; never shorten a hold.
- Offer + CTA ≤ 25% of the length.
- One datum is carried from input to result.
- The cut keeps the master's pattern and only its result moment.

| Pattern | Hook | Close | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem → result | The pain, in the audience's own artifact | Old vs new in one frame | Problem-aware; a visible workaround |
| Result-first | The finished result | Return to the hook frame | The result reads at phone size |
| Before / after | The before state | A split for ≤ 2 s | Both fit one screen |
| Question → answer | The question, with the real input | The answer is the product | Unaware an app does this |
| Watch this | "Watch: [input] → [result]" | One continuous take | Real-time speed is the claim; otherwise label it "sped up" |
| Listicle-of-one | "One [tool] every [who] needs for [task]" | As problem → result | Creator-native TikTok or Reels |

Muted-first placements (4:5, 1:1) use a picture hook, never a question. Second-language and code-switched audiences get the hook's meaning in the picture, with no wordplay.

### Hook

| Type | Frame 0 | First line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Call-out | Their world, their label | "[Who], still [workaround]?" |
| Pain shown | The workaround failing | The pain in their words |
| Result shown | The finished result | The result in the screen's words |
| Number | A real number from **Proof**, big | The number and what it costs them |
| Contrast | Two states of one task | "[Old]: [time]. [New]: [time]." |
| Question | The real input | "What if this [input] became [result]?" |

- Frame 0 reads in 0.25 s. It is never black and never a logo alone; a branded frame 0 or a staying watermark is fine.
- ≤ 7 words on screen before 3.0 s, hook text and label together.
- The first line starts at 0.1–0.3 s, ends ≤ 2.8 s, and never opens with "Introducing".
- Write 3 hooks of different types, each ending on the same frame at 3.0 ± 0.1 s, so the body after it is identical.

### Lines

| Line | Job | Window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Audience, pain or result | 0.1–2.8 s |
| Sold | "[Name] [verb]s [result] for [who]"; when sold ≠ shown, "We build [category] like this for [who]" | ≤ 3 s, over a still frame, ending ≤ 6.5 s |
| Moment | Names the change with the **anchor**, the screen's own word for it | Lands within −0.1 / +0.3 s of the change |
| Restate | Brand + anchor phrase | ≤ 2.5 s |
| CTA | Verb + destination + offer | ≤ 2.5 s (≤ 1.7 s in the cut) |

- One idea per line: one sentence of 1.5–3.5 s that never crosses a moment boundary; ≤ 1 claim per line.
- The anchor stays in the UI's language, even mid code-switch ("I-tap ang parts, total agad" where the UI says "Total").
- Short of time → say the consequence, not the step.
- A line's slot = its syllables ÷ the voice's measured rate; use 3.5 syllables a second until the voice is measured. A 30 s master holds about 55–75 syllables.
- ≤ 7 lines in the master, ≤ 4 in the cut.
- Quiet: ≥ 0.4 s between lines; after each proof moment (**Holds**); through every camera move; while the product's own sound plays; at the end card's tail (**End card**).
- Pauses are gaps between separate takes, written `[quiet 1.2]`. Inside a line, use only commas and full stops.

### Voice, screen and caption

| Channel | Carries | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voice | Meaning (result + consequence), the sold line, the CTA | Anything not on screen, except the hook's pain and the CTA |
| Screen | One change at a time, and its proof | Proof that can't be read in its window |
| Label | ≤ 4 words in the app's language; its noun is the anchor; appears with the change, touches it, holds to the moment's end | A sentence |
| Caption | The line's key phrase, 2–5 words, still once it appears | Word-by-word over UI; repeating or covering the label |

- **Caption rule:** every voiced line's key phrase appears on screen once, as the label in proof windows and as a caption elsewhere. Verbatim captions only when nothing on screen needs reading. No voice → no captions.
- **Reading budget per window:** words the voice says are free; a number counts as 2 words. With a caption: N_read ≤ (0.6 × settled seconds − T_gist) ÷ t_word; without one, drop the 0.6. T_gist = 1.1 s fluent, 1.4 s older second-language, +0.4 s after a hard cut; t_word from **Holds**.
- **Per language:** reading rate from Brysbaert 2019, Table 5 (English 236 words a minute, Finnish 195, Arabic 181). Caption speed and line length from Netflix's timed-text guide for the caption language (English 20 characters a second, German 17, Korean 12, Chinese 9, Japanese 4 with 13 full-width characters a line). Chinese, Japanese and Thai are budgeted in characters.

### Claims

A claim is a distinct pain, capability, benefit, number, guarantee, price or term, counted when voiced, captioned, labelled or titled. A repeat isn't a new claim; incidental UI text isn't counted but must be true.

| Length | Claims |
| --- | --- |
| ≤ 15 s | 3: pain, outcome, offer |
| 21–25 s | 4 |
| 26–34 s | 5 |

- Never more than 2 per 10 s. One fewer for SMB, mass-market and second-language audiences.
- The master holds 1 pain, the outcome, ≤ 2 supports each proven inside a moment, and 1 offer.
- Cut in this order: claims outside the one task; claims with no picture at that moment; second offer terms; pains beyond the first; unmeasured adjectives ("fast"); a support that competes with the outcome. Never cut the outcome, the sold line, the offer or the CTA.

### Offer and CTA

- One offer of ≤ 6 words: "Download [Name] free" (the cold default), "First 10 [units] on us", a guarantee from **Proof**, or a price only when the price is the hook. Terms go to the landing page or the reply.
- The spoken verb is the button's verb. The brand is spoken ≥ 2×.

| Destination | Spoken | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| App store | "Download [Name] free" | Store badges |
| Website | "Try it free at [short domain]" | URL |
| Organic TikTok / Instagram | "Link in bio" | Handle |
| Enterprise | "Book a 15-minute demo" | "Book a demo" |

### Moment

One task step, in up to 10 steps:

| Step | Job | Length | Optional |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Establish | Whole device or window; nothing read | 0.8–1.5 s | Skip when the last moment ended here, and in the cut |
| 2 Isolate | One push-in to the reading crop | 0.5–0.6 s | No |
| 3 Settle | The eye refixes and takes the gist | ≥ 0.4 s fluent; ≥ 0.6 s SMB or second-language | No |
| 4 Before | The old state, held still | ≥ 0.6 s (overlaps 3) | No |
| 5 Cue | Dim, outline or cursor arrives | 0.3 s, ending ≥ 0.2 s before step 6 | Merges into 3 when the framing is the cue |
| 6 Trigger | The pressed state on touch-down; fires on release | 0.1–0.15 s | Skip for system events |
| 7 Change | The product's own animation | 0.1–0.4 s; a typed or counted value 0.6–1.0 s | No |
| 8 Name | The label lands as the voice says the anchor | Within ±0.3 s of step 7 | No |
| 9 After-hold | Still, from **Holds** | 1.8–7.5 s | No |
| 10 Return or carry | Shows where the result lives | 0.5–0.6 s | Only for a new screen, or a result that moves |

A moment on a new screen gets steps 1–10; on the same screen, steps 2–9; the cut's moment, steps 2–9.

- The before-state is the screen rebuilt from the source. The after-state comes from the next screen in the source or its sample data; with neither, it isn't shown.
- One change per moment, plus at most one consequence with its own window. The app section changes state ≤ 0.35 times a second.

| Change | Animate | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A value fills | Typed (about 15 characters a second), or counts up | 0.6–1.0 s, ease-out |
| A row appears | Grows from height 0; rows below shift; content fades in | 0.3 s, then 0.15 s |
| A status flips | The chip's colour and text cross-fade to the product's status token | 0.2 s |
| A sheet slides | translateY 100% → 0, with the product's scrim | 0.3–0.35 s |
| A screen pushes | The next screen slides in the way the source navigates | 0.35 s |
| A list fills | 40–80 ms stagger, counted as one event | ≤ 0.8 s |
| An AI result | A working state ≥ 0.6 s, a dissolve, then the result | — |

### Framing

- **Read band** (9:16, inside the safe zones): x 65–1015, y 269–1248, so 950 × 979 px. A 4:5 frame minus 6% sides is also 950 px wide.
- **Text sizes:** meaning-bearing text reaches ≥ 38 px in the video; 29 px is the floor; under 19 px can't be read. A whole 390-wide phone fits the band only at k ≤ 1.16, so it never carries reading.

| Framing | k (video px per source px) | What reads | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole device in its world | ≤ 1.16 | Text ≥ 26 px only | Establish, hook, return, end card |
| Push-in crop | 38 ÷ the smallest meaning-bearing font px (never below 29 ÷ it) | Real UI | Every proof moment |
| Lifted card, device dimmed | As push-in | Real UI | A result that travels, or two sources compared |
| Simplified screen | The product's own strings at 77–120 px | 3–4 big words | Gist claims; low-tech audiences |
| Desktop | Window 0.66 → pane about 1.2 → component 2.7–2.9 | The component only | Two-stage push |
| Before/after split | Each half ≥ 38 px | 2 labels + 2 values | Old vs new |

- **Crop:** f = the smallest font px among the meaning-bearing strings (on a chart, the delta and the shape, not the ticks); k = 38 ÷ f; crop 950 ÷ k × 979 ÷ k around the change, the change in the central half. A crop that doesn't fit → cut proof words or split the moment; never lower k.
- **Choose:** push-in when the viewer must read; whole device or simplified when the moment is voiced and nothing must be read. Simplify for low-tech, second-language or 60+ viewers, for evidence that won't fit a 38 px crop, and for cold vertical campaigns. Real UI when viewers judge by the UI (dev and pro), the claim is about the UI, or a number or output is the proof.
- **Product tokens stay exact.** A proof string under 4.5:1 contrast in the product's colours → pick another proof string or let the label carry it.

### Camera

| Move | Duration | Tells the viewer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Push-in anchored on the target | 0.5–0.6 s ease in-out; above a 2.5× scale change, 0.7–0.8 s or two moves | Closer, same place |
| Pull-back | 0.5–0.6 s | Where it lives |
| Pan within one screen | 0.4–0.6 s | Next to it |
| Carry | 0.5–0.65 s | It goes there |
| Punch-in (a cut) | 0 | Look here |
| Whip, iris, flash | 0.13–0.6 s | Energy |

- One move in and at most one out per moment, none during steps 6–9; a desktop two-stage push counts as one move in.
- Push-in by default; carry only along a real data path; whip, iris and flash only between worlds. Older and second-language viewers get the long end of each range.
- Move (with motion blur) → dead still for the settle → act → still through the hold and the quiet second → next move.

### Cues

| Cue | Settings | When | Registry block |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Framing (target centred) | — | Always first | `ui-focus-zoom`, halo hidden, zoom from **Framing**, its 1.1 s move shortened |
| Dim the rest | Ground-ink overlay at 40–50%; rounded-rect cutout 12–16 px larger than the target; 0.3 s | The context must stay visible | `yt-feather-highlight`, reshaped from an ellipse |
| Outline or highlight | State colour or the overlay accent, 3–4 px, drawn in 0.25 s | A row or phrase the voice names | `outline-draw`, `inline-highlight` |
| Label with a leader line | ≤ 4 words, 44–56 px, on a solid pill; a 2 px line ≤ 200 px drawn in 0.2 s, ending in a dot at the target's edge | Every proof change | Hand-built |
| Tap disc (mobile) | Translucent, no taller than the control, ≤ 35% fill, on the control's end | Changes the user causes | `touch-indicator` |
| Cursor (desktop) | The real cursor × k (4–7% of frame width); one eased 0.5–0.8 s path, stopping ≥ 0.3 s before the click | Desktop UIs | `oversized-cursor` |

- Avoid `press-ripple`, `agent-cursor`, `cursor-zoom-follow` (click rings, a trailing camera), `hw-callout-circle`, `hw-box-label` (text moves while read), `device-frame-stage`, `multi-device-splay` (idle float; freeze it), `pan-stations`, `sticky-mock-swap`, `screen-flow-carousel` (feature tours) and `logo-outro` (glow).
- One cue per change, cheapest first: framing, outline, dim, label. It overlaps its target (IoU ≥ 0.5), arrives after the settle, ends ≥ 0.2 s before the trigger, and leaves with the moment. The label sits ≤ 150 px from its target, never on it.

### Holds

```
before ≥ max(0.6, m_age·(C + m_app·O))
after  = m_age·[m_app·F + m_lang·Σ(words·m_size)/4 + m_dom·I] ÷ (1 − s_cap)
then   1.0 s with no new voice or caption after a proof moment
```

| Term | Value |
| --- | --- |
| C, cost of a cut | 0.4 s after a hard cut, whip or flash; 0.2 s after a move the eye can follow; 0 for a change in place |
| O, gist of the screen | 0.3 s; +0.2 s for a busy or new layout |
| F, finding the change | 0.3 s guided; unguided 0.4 s + 0.125 s per item |
| Reading | Unvoiced words only, at the reading language's rate; a number counts as 2 words |
| I, meaning | 0.5 s when the voice says the consequence; 1.0 s to link two things or when the result isn't voiced; 1.5 s to compare or choose |
| s_cap, caption pull | 0.4 while a caption appears; 0 with a label only |
| m_size, by x-height | ≥ 0.20° ×1.0 · 0.15–0.20° ×1.15 · 0.12–0.15° ×1.4 · 0.10–0.12° ×1.8 (38 px ≈ 0.20°, 29 px ≈ 0.15°) |
| m_lang | 1.1–1.25 for second-language app text |
| m_age | 1.0 (under 30) · 1.1 (30–49) · 1.15 (50–59) · 1.3–1.5 (60+) |
| m_app | 1.0–1.3, from the audience's digital literacy |
| m_dom | 0.85 for the viewer's own trade; 1.2 for an unfamiliar AI step |

| Seconds per unvoiced word | 38 px | 29 px | 22 px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fluent | 0.25 | 0.29 | 0.45 |
| Moderate (30–55 professional, daily app user) | 0.30 | 0.35 | 0.54 |
| Low-tech or second-language (35–60 owner, non-app user) | 0.36 | 0.41 | 0.65 |

- A gist moment (voiced, nothing unvoiced to read) needs an after-hold of about 1.8–2.8 s.
- ≤ 4 unvoiced words per after-window for SMB and second-language viewers; ≤ 6 for fluent viewers.
- No hold shrinks to fit a beat; a seam moves onto a beat only when it's within 2 frames. Long holds carry `data-hold`.
- Too fast means longer holds and fewer changes, never slower zooms.

### Seams

| Seam | Tells | Use | Never |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pull-back → push-in | Same app, another part | Moments on one screen | — |
| The product's own navigation at its own timing | This tap opens that | Screen changes | Navigation the product doesn't have |

| Hard cut | A new place or time | Moments on different screens | Inside a moment; mid-read |
| Through an object; colour flood | A new world | Old way → app; app → offer | Inside the app |
| Whip, iris, flash | Energy | Between worlds | Inside the task |

Keep one home framing (the whole device in a fixed place) and return to it at every screen change. Use 2–3 seam kinds in the app section, each with a written meaning; every in-app seam maps to a flow step or a real data flow. Nothing changes in the 0.4 s after a hard cut.

### Type, colour and placement

| Role | 9:16 px | 4:5 px | Per line | Lines |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title / hook | 96–150 | 84–120 | ≤ 5 words and ≤ 950 px in the rendered font | ≤ 2 |
| Caption (key phrase) | 64–76 | 56–68 | ≤ 5 words, ≤ 24–27 characters | ≤ 2 |
| Label | 44–56 | 44–52 | ≤ 4 words | 1 |
| UI text that must be read, after k | ≥ 38 (floor 29) | Same | — | — |
| Sample badge | ≥ 29 | ≥ 29 | — | 1 |
| End card: name / offer / CTA | 80–110 / 56–72 / 48–60; button ≥ 120 px tall | ×0.9 | — | — |

- Size runs title > caption > label ≥ UI text. Older and second-language viewers get the upper sizes and fewer words.
- Line width is measured in px in the rendered font; long compounds get soft hyphens with the right `lang`. Right-to-left languages right-align and mirror the layout. Scripts without capitals use weight and size where the tone says caps.
- Every read string has ≥ 4.5:1 contrast (3:1 only for text ≥ 72 px). HyperFrames' check gates 3:1 from 24 px, so pick the overlay's text and ground at ≥ 4.5:1 from their hexes.
- The change shows in the product's own state colour. The seller's brand appears only in the seller's world, the badge and the end card. Overlay cues use one accent per frame, ≥ 3:1 against both the UI and the ground.

| Element | Where (9:16) | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Upper middle of the band, y 420–900 | Hook, world changes, offer; never while UI is read |
| Caption | A fixed band at x 72, y 330–520, left-aligned | Outside proof windows |
| Label | Beside the change, away from the next change, ≤ 150 px from it | Steps 8–9 |
| Badge | Top right of the band, y ≈ 272 | Every frame that carries the label |

- No text over the change or its label; no caption over a dimmed screen. HyperFrames' default portrait captions sit in Meta's bottom 35%, so they move to the caption band.
- `npx hyperframes check --caption-zone` flags text that overlaps the zone it's given, so pass each unsafe zone in its own run (Reels: `x0=0;y0=0;x1=1;y1=.14`, `x0=0;y0=.65;x1=1;y1=1`, `x0=0;y0=0;x1=.06;y1=1`, `x0=.94;y0=0;x1=1;y1=1`; TikTok: its wider right rail). Right-to-left languages mirror the positions.

### End card

| Layout | Holds | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Result + CTA | The payoff screen pulled back to the whole device in the upper half; offer line; CTA; seller mark | SMB, consumer, cold |
| Lockup | Mark, name, one line, CTA | Pro / dev |
| Store badges | `store-badge-lockup` | Installable apps |

1. Enter through the last object: the result shrinks into place.
2. The mark lands once, in 0.4–0.6 s.
3. The offer, then the CTA, 0.3 s apart.
4. The CTA voice starts ≤ 0.3 s in. The last ≥ 1.5 s is dead still with no voice, while the music leads.
5. 3–5 s in total, ≤ 10 unvoiced words.

### Voice

| Register | Fits |
| --- | --- |
| Peer, conversational | Consumer, SMB, low-tech, local markets |
| Warm guide | Utilities, health, finance, education |
| Measured authority | Enterprise, security, finance, dev launches |
| Energetic host | Events, youth, hype |
| No voice (music + type) | Music-led films |

- The audience's everyday spoken language, code-switched the way they talk, in their region's accent. One narrator. A second voice only when the product hears speech: a different timbre, labelled as a sample, one 2–4 s line with the narrator silent.

**Casting** (the test is a paid call):

1. Write a casting line: language and code-switch, accent, register, age impression, warmth ↔ authority.
2. Search the ElevenLabs Voice Library by Language → Accent, Category (Advertisement, Narration, Social Media, Conversational), Gender, Age and Quality "Studio"; prefer voices with a notice period.
3. Shortlist 3 voices and render the same 3 test lines on each: the hook; the result line with a number and the product name; the CTA.
4. The user listens dry, on a phone speaker at low volume, and keeps a voice that says names and numbers right, falls in the pace band, a native speaker approves (the user or someone they name), and fits the tone.
5. Record the voice id, settings and reason in **Voice**; reuse them across a series.

**Delivery.** `eleven_v4` has no speed control or SSML; steer it by text:

| Lever | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Comma (0.3–0.6 s) | A breath inside a line |
| Full stop / question mark (0.4–0.6 s) | Sentence end; question hook |
| Ellipsis | Before the result word, at most once a line |
| CAPS on one word | The result word |
| One audio tag (`[curious]`) | At most one per line |
| Stability | From the tone row |
| `previous_text` / `next_text` | Every take, for continuous tone |

- **Pace band:** 0.70–0.90 × the language's normal rate, pauses removed; the tone row sets where. English 4.3–5.6 syllables a second, French 5.0–6.5, German 4.2–5.4, Italian 4.9–6.3, Spanish 5.5–7.0, Japanese 5.5–7.1, Mandarin 3.6–4.7. Other languages: measure the chosen voice on the test lines and never go faster than it naturally speaks. Older, second-language and low-tech viewers get the low end.
- The result word goes at the end of its line.

**Takes:**

1. One request per line to `POST https://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/text-to-speech/<voice_id>/with-timestamps`, with `text` (numbers written as spoken), `model_id`, `language_code`, `voice_settings`, `previous_text` and `next_text`. Save the audio and the `alignment` JSON (`characters`, `character_start_times_seconds`, `character_end_times_seconds`) per line in `assets/vo/raw/`, with the inputs in `lines.json`.
2. Render 2–3 takes of the hook, each result line and the CTA; the user picks by ear.
3. Compute each line's rate from its alignment.
4. Place each take on its picture anchor with the gaps from **Lines**. A line that doesn't fit → shorten the line or hold the picture longer.
5. Trim only trailing silence. Never time-stretch or change the playback rate.

### Music

| Role | When | Level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads | A hook with no voice, voice gaps ≥ 2 s, the end card's tail, music-only films | Bed + 4–8 dB (default +6) |
| Under the voice | Every voiced line | Per **Mix** |
| Near silence | The old-way world | Optional |
| A sung jingle as the message | Local-mass, with budget for a custom song | No voiceover over it |

- Genre and tempo come from the tone row. Voice-led films get a soft pulse: no busy hi-hats or snare runs, no vocals under speech.
- The voice and the holds time the picture; the music is edited to them. HyperFrames' beat-cut defaults apply only to music-only films.
- **Energy:** audible from frame 0, fading in over ≤ 0.4 s; a section change within one beat of the product reveal; one steady section through the moments, with no new instruments under a result line and rises only in voice gaps ≥ 2 s; the fullest section at the offer, still at bed level under the offer line; the music leads at the end and its last downbeat lands on the mark.

| Source | Cost | Made by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A bank bed, logo or sting ([marketing-contract.md](./marketing-contract.md) → **Sound bank**) | Free | **Re-cut** |
| ElevenLabs music (`music_v2_5`) | Paid, by length | **Compose**, after the picture lock |
| None | — | — |

**Re-cut: a bank bed to this timeline.**

1. Run `npx hyperframes audio <bed> --only beats,downbeats,bpm,sections --json`. Read `bpm` with `beats.confidence`, and accept half or double the tone's tempo; a free-tempo bed is read by the plan's section times in its sidecar.
2. Map the bed's sections to the timeline and cut only on bar lines, at repeating material, with a 20–50 ms crossfade. It fits when every section change lands within one beat of its mark. Keep only the cut in `assets/music.<ext>`, and its recipe in `SPEC.md`.
3. The bed covers the whole film. On a rhythmic bed the last downbeat lands within 2 frames of the mark.

**Compose: ElevenLabs music to the locked timeline.**

1. Read the beat times the placed takes lock, and the hit list in **Music**.
2. Pick a tempo in the tone row's BPM range that puts every hit on a downbeat; when none does, lengthen a hold, never shorten one.
3. Write a composition plan with one chunk per section of the energy arc (hook + sold line; the moments; the offer; the end card), each starting on its hit. Chunks run 3–120 s, ≤ 30 a plan, so a shorter beat joins its neighbour. `/v1/music/plan` drafts one free.
4. The first chunk carries 6–7 positive styles from the tone row: genre, instruments, tempo, mood, "instrumental". Every chunk's negative styles include vocals, busy hi-hats and snare runs; a sung jingle drops "vocals" and runs its lyrics through **Claims** and G1. Never name an artist, band or song.
5. Compose once at the film's length with `music_v2_5` and a `seed`, per the music skill.
6. `music_v2_5` holds each chunk's length; check the section changes and downbeats with `npx hyperframes audio`, and cut a change more than one beat off its mark at a bar line (**Re-cut** step 2).
7. Record the plan and seed in **Music**, and the length under **Paid calls**.

### Sound effects

| Kind | Only on |
| --- | --- |
| Soft tap or click | A real tap that causes the next change |
| Result (a soft pop, stamp or settle; never a tone) | The one result in each moment |
| Short whoosh | A world change or a big carry; ≤ 2 per film, different takes |
| Riser → hit | Once, into the payoff or the mark; never under a line |
| Foley (paper, pen, a phone set down) | Real objects in the old-way world |
| Ambience | Under the old way, ≥ 15 LU below the voice |
| Keys | Visible typing at its real speed |

- **Named changes only:** each moment's result, the tap that causes it, a world change, the mark. Never rows or list items landing, counters, rings, captions, cursor or camera moves, scrolls.
- **Cap:** ≤ 2 per moment, ≤ 0.4 a second, ≤ 12 in a master and ≤ 6 in the cut (the tone row narrows it), starts ≥ 0.5 s apart.
- **Level and timing:** the peak sits +3 to +6 dB over the bed and ≥ 6 dB under any voice it overlaps; it lands 0–1 frame after the picture.
- **Never:** chimes, dings, pings, notification tones, sparkles, runs of ticks, the same whoosh on every cut, impacts under a line, real platform notification sounds.
- Pick one material per kind, with one take per use, up to 3 to rotate; a file's hit offset is `moments.peak` from `npx hyperframes audio <file> --json`.

### Mix

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Voice | Each take −16 LUFS, measured as played (a mono take upmixed at unity) |
| Bed under the voice | Voice − 15 LU for consumer, SMB, vertical, older or second-language viewers; voice − 12 LU for pro and dev launches |
| Floor | ≥ 10 LU on every line |
| Lead | Bed + 6 dB (4–8), only in voice gaps ≥ 2 s |
| Duck | Down 0.2 s before the first word; up from 0.3 s after the last word, over 0.8 s; down through gaps < 1.5 s |
| Carve | media-use `carve.mjs` on the bed; it also writes its own duck on the bed, so measure each line's floor after it |
| Master | −14 ± 1 LUFS integrated; true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; limiter last |

Normalize takes and bed (upmix mono first) → set the bed from the gap, never by a raw volume number → write the duck as a volume lane → carve → add effects → limiter at −1 dBTP → measure the render with ffmpeg `ebur128=peak=true` and scale every element's `data-volume` by the gap to −14 LUFS → listen on a phone speaker at low volume.

### HyperFrames brief

HyperFrames follows the script and storyboard it's given but writes its own `SPEC.md`, where anything unset falls back to its defaults. So it gets **Script**, **Scenes** and this list, and its `SPEC.md` is checked for the holds, sizes and banned list before it builds.

**Spec fields:** `message`, `audience`, `arc`, `tone` (the row), `clock` (`voice`, or `music` for a jingle), `audience_multipliers` (m_age, m_app, m_lang, m_dom), `unsafe_zones`, `type_scale`, `color_roles`, `source` (codebase or mockup), `banned`.

**Scenes fields, per moment:**

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| `src`, `duration`, `transition_in`, `voiceover`, `reads`, `sound` | HyperFrames' storyboard keys; `reads` = the before, change and after windows |
| `shot` | `list item appears and is flagged` |
| `screen`, `after_from` | `02-crew.html`; `03-flags.html#flag-1` |
| `change` | `insert #row-jun; .chip → st-red "Also Site B"` |
| `framing`, `region`, `k`, `min_text_px` | `push-in`; `#crew-list 0,120,390,360`; `2.4`; `38 (floor 29)` |
| `camera`, `settle`, `before` | `in: push 0.55s @#row-jun; out: none`; `0.6s`; `0.8s` |
| `cue` | `outline #row-jun 3px; dim rest 45%` |
| `trigger` | `tap #release, press 0.12s, fires on release, at w23` |
| `notice` | What the viewer must take away |
| `label`, `voice_anchor` | `"Also on Site B" right, leader 120px`; `"Site B" w27 ±0.3s` |
| `hold_after`, `quiet_after` | `5.3s data-hold`; `1.0s` |
| `changes_max`, `caption` | `1 (+1 consequence)`; `label-as-caption` |
| `seam_out` | `pull-back 0.5s: back to the crew list` |

**Banned:** vertical 1–2 s shots; "the process runs fast"; a new layout every bar; micro-timing to the beat; click rings; idle float; glow; bounce on UI; text that moves while it's read; chime, ding, ping and notification effects; word-by-word captions over UI; a whole phone used for reading.

A `text_below_floor` or `line_unread` warning on a proof element blocks the draft.

### Checks

| Gate | When | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 Cold read | After **Script**, before any paid call | A fresh subagent sees only **Script** with its labels and captions, then the hook alone, and says what is sold, to whom, what it fixes and what to do next. Its answers match the core line |
| G2 Budget | Before the build | Per moment from **Scenes**: the computed hold vs the planned window; font px × k (< 29 fails, < 38 warns); unvoiced words ≤ N_read; ≤ 3 moments; claims ≤ the cap; the offer share |
| G3 Measured | On every draft and the final | Per moment: the settled window by frame differencing with the overlay layer hidden; one change window; no change during a move; ≥ 1 s of quiet after proofs; state changes per second. Per film: length; text sizes and line widths; each unsafe zone through `--caption-zone`; proof strings legible on HyperFrames' 360 px phone sheet; effect count and spacing; `grep -E "chime|ding|ping|notification|sparkle"` on the effect files finds nothing. Final only: anchors within −0.1 / +0.3 s from the alignment; joins ≥ 0.4 s by `silencedetect`; no `atempo` or playback-rate change on the voice; the voice gap on voice-only and music-only renders (the other tracks' `data-volume` at 0); the duck; `ebur128=peak=true` reads −15 to −13 LUFS and a peak ≤ −1.0; every crop ratio keeps each label, caption and control inside the crop |
| G4 Real viewers (optional) | Offered once after the final, before posting; never blocks | The skill writes one recall question per moment plus "what is it, who is it for, what would you do next". 5–8 people from the audience who haven't seen the Brief each watch once on their own phone; the user brings back the answers, and a moment most of them miss is fixed and re-rendered |

A G1–G3 failure is fixed before the user sees the draft; the reply reports what each gate found.
